## 5. Responsible use

Three rules governed the use of AI tools, and they are the reason the log can be
specific.

1. **Nothing was accepted on the assistant's word.** AI-written code was run against the
   project's data, and its output compared with a figure computed by another route. The
   dashboard is the clearest case: the assistant built it, and it was then checked by
   querying the loaded model and subtracting the R pipeline's own numbers from it.
2. **The assistant was corrected when it was wrong.** It kept a map from the first
   iteration that was bound to the wrong country's geography (E4-02), and its first draft
   of report sections 5 and 7 overran the page budget. Both are recorded above rather
   than quietly fixed.
3. **Personal and evaluative writing stays personal.** The reflection was redrafted by
   its author (AI-M4-C6), and the ethics decisions in section 2 record judgements the
   team made, written down with assistance rather than decided by it.

## 6. Reflection - Timothy Wubbeling (Person C)

> **Draft for Timothy to rewrite.** See AI-M4-C6. A reflection in someone else's words
> is not a reflection; use this only as a prompt for your own, and keep it to 150-200
> words.

Milestone 4 was the first time the whole project had to agree with itself. My job was not new analysis but making the dashboard, the app and the report quote one set of numbers. Two correct referral rates, 58.9% on the test set and 59.2% across all households, could have looked like a mistake; the fix was to label both rather than hide one.

What surprised me was how much of the merge was judgement rather than technique. My Milestone 1 dashboard read the raw files, so bolting its pages on would have put pre-cleaning numbers beside final ones; rebuilding them on the integrated data was slower and the only honest option. I also removed a map I had been proud of, which was bound to a United States shape file and had never drawn South African provinces.

What changed is where I think accuracy comes from. In Milestone 2 I trusted a clean notebook; now I trust a figure only when two independent paths produce it, which is why the dashboard subtracts the R output from itself in front of the user.

What I would improve: I left reconciliation until the dashboard existed. Writing the check first would have caught drift while it was still cheap.
